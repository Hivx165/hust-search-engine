      HUST Co-organises International Conference on Industrial–Organizational Psychology in the Digital Era

      Viện Sư phạm Kỹ thuật
      2026-10-16T17:11:05+07:00 2026-10-16T17:11:05+07:00 https://fed.hust.edu.vn/en/news/news/hust-co-organises-international-conference-on-industrial-organizational-psychology-in-the-digital-era-15-html-15.html https://fed.hust.edu.vn/uploads/feed/news/2026_10/assoc_prof_dao_giang_phat_bieu_khai_mac_compressed.jpg

      On October 10, 2026, Hanoi University of Science and Technology (HUST), in collaboration with Dai Nam University, co-organised the 2026 International Conference on Industrial - Organizational Psychology in the Digital Era. The conference brought together scholars, researchers, and practitioners from Vietnam and around the world to explore the increasingly important role of Industrial - Organizational Psychology (I-O Psychology) in addressing the challenges of digital transformation and the changing world of work.
      Hosted by Dai Nam University in Hanoi, the conference featured keynote presentations and parallel sessions covering a wide range of topics, including artificial intelligence and organisational change, leadership, employee selection and assessment, workplace well-being, and human performance in the digital era.
      Putting people at the centre of digital transformation
      In her opening remarks, Assoc. Prof. Dr. Dao Thi Thu Giang, Rector of Dai Nam University, highlighted the importance of maintaining a human-centred perspective amid rapid technological advancement.
      “As technology becomes more intelligent, how can we make our organizations MORE HUMAN?” she asked, emphasising that successful organisational transformation depends not only on technological capabilities but also on leadership, trust, motivation, adaptability, and employee well-being.
      She further stressed the responsibility of universities to prepare students for an evolving world of work by developing not only professional and digital competencies but also critical thinking, creativity, emotional intelligence, and ethical responsibility.
      Representing HUST as a co-organiser, Assoc. Prof. Dr. Le Hieu Hoc, Dean of the Faculty of Education, emphasised the strategic importance of I-O Psychology in today's rapidly changing and uncertain world, particularly in Vietnam's transition towards an innovation-driven and digitally enabled economy.
      “Digital transformation is not only about technology. It is fundamentally about people and organisations,” he stated.
      He noted that I-O Psychology can contribute significantly to developing an adaptable and resilient workforce, supporting effective leadership, enhancing employee engagement, and creating productive and psychologically healthy workplaces.
      HUST's distinctive approach: Integrating psychology, data science, and artificial intelligence
      A key highlight of HUST's contribution is its interdisciplinary approach to I-O Psychology, drawing on the University's strengths in engineering, information technology, mathematics, and data science.
      Assoc. Prof. Le Hieu Hoc highlighted the potential of people analytics, predictive analytics, and artificial intelligence to enhance personnel assessment, competency development, talent management, and evidence-based organisational decision-making.
      He also emphasised the importance of responsible applications of these technologies: “Data can help us understand patterns in human behaviour, but it cannot replace human judgement, empathy, and ethical responsibility.”
      Through its Faculty of Education, HUST is developing education and research in I-O Psychology with a strong focus on data-driven approaches and practical applications. The year 2026 also marks the first intake of HUST's Bachelor's Programme in Industrial and Organisational Psychology, representing an important step in the University's commitment to this emerging interdisciplinary field.
      International perspectives on AI, leadership, and organisational transformation
      The conference's plenary session, themed “Artificial Intelligence and Organizational Change Management in the Digital Workplace,” featured two keynote presentations exploring the human dimensions of technological and organisational transformation.
      Dr. Sydney Engelberg from the Hebrew University of Jerusalem, Israel, delivered a keynote presentation entitled “Reprogramming Organizational Habits: A Cognitive and Behavioral Science Perspective on Change Management in the Age of AI.” His presentation addressed organisational change through the lens of cognitive and behavioural science, highlighting the importance of understanding established behavioural patterns when organisations adapt to technological transformation.
      Dr. Pham Kim Anh from the University of Social Sciences and Humanities, Vietnam National University Ho Chi Minh City, presented “From Power to Psychological Safety: Reimagining Leadership in the Digital Workplace.” Her presentation focused on leadership and psychological safety, drawing attention to the importance of creating working environments that encourage trust, participation, and employee well-being amid digital transformation.
      Together, the two keynote topics reflected the conference's central message: successful digital transformation requires not only technological innovation but also a deeper understanding of human behaviour, leadership, and organisational dynamics.
      Parallel sessions explore emerging challenges in the digital workplace
      Following the plenary session, the conference continued with two parallel sessions featuring presentations by international and Vietnamese scholars and practitioners.
      The first session, “Employee Selection, Assessment, Health and Well-being in the Age of AI,” focused on the application of psychological science and emerging technologies to human resource management and employee well-being. Presentations addressed people analytics and data-driven decision-making, advanced personnel assessment tools, psychometric approaches to employee assessment, and employees' perceptions of technological job displacement and its implications for workplace well-being. Notably, Assoc. Prof. Dr. Pham Manh Ha from HUST presented research on subscale-level psychometric evidence and prerequisites for transferring the Career Passport Digital Assessment System from career guidance to employee assessment.
      The second session, “Leadership, Work and Human Performance in the Digital Era,” explored organisational behaviour, work motivation, and leadership in digitally transforming environments. Presentations examined human-centred approaches to organisational effectiveness, leadership as an interaction between leaders, followers and their contexts, organisational responses to parental educational anxiety in high-pressure school systems, psychological well-being among women working in India's informal sector, and the use of digital technologies in psychological training for athletes.
      Across both sessions, the contributions highlighted the expanding scope of I-O Psychology and its relevance to contemporary challenges in workforce development, organisational effectiveness, employee well-being, and human performance. The diversity of topics and international perspectives also demonstrated the potential for interdisciplinary research and collaboration, particularly at the intersection of psychology, management, education, and digital technologies.
      Towards the establishment of SIOP Vietnam
      Another significant highlight of the conference's opening ceremony was the launch of the Liaison Committee for the Establishment of the Society for Industrial and Organizational Psychology (SIOP) Vietnam.
      This initiative represents an important step towards building a professional and academic community for I-O Psychology in Vietnam. It is expected to facilitate networking, knowledge exchange, research collaboration, and stronger connections between universities, researchers, and organisations interested in applying psychological science to workplace challenges.
      The initiative also reflects the growing recognition of I-O Psychology as an important field supporting workforce development, organisational effectiveness, and employee well-being in Vietnam.
      Strengthening academic collaboration and real-world impact
      The conference provided a valuable platform for exchanging research findings, sharing international experiences, and exploring opportunities for collaboration between academia and industry.
      For HUST, the event also demonstrated its commitment to advancing interdisciplinary education and research that connect technological innovation with a deeper understanding of human behaviour and organisational development.
      As HUST celebrates its 70th anniversary in October 2026, its participation in the conference further reinforces the University's efforts to develop partnerships and contribute to emerging areas of scientific research and professional practice.
      Concluding his remarks, Assoc. Prof. Le Hieu Hoc shared a message that captured the spirit of the conference: “The future of work will certainly be shaped by technology. But the quality of that future will be determined by how well we understand, develop, and care for people.”
      The conference reaffirmed a shared commitment to ensuring that technological advancement and organisational transformation remain fundamentally human-centred.
