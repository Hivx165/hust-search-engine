      ICEF 2026 BRINGS TOGETHER NEARLY 300 INTERNATIONAL AND LOCAL SCHOLARS TO ADVANCE EDUCATIONAL INNOVATION AND LEARNER EMPOWERMENT

      Faculty of Education (FED) | Hanoi University of Science and Technology (HUST)

      The International Conference on Educational Sciences and Foreign Language Teaching (ICEF 2026) successfully took place from October 22 to 24, 2026, in Ninh Binh, Vietnam, bringing together nearly 300 scholars, researchers, educators, and academic professionals from around the world. Under the theme “Empowering Learners in the Digital Global Era: Insights, Innovations, and Impact,” the conference explored how educational research, emerging technologies, and international collaboration can contribute to more inclusive, innovative, and impactful learning experiences.
      Organised by Hanoi University of Science and Technology (HUST), with the Faculty of Education (FED) and the Faculty of Foreign Languages (FFL) playing leading roles, ICEF 2026 marked the third consecutive edition of the international academic conference.
      The conference welcomed participants from the United States, Canada, India, China, South Korea, the Philippines, Hong Kong, Vietnam, and other countries. Participation increased substantially from approximately 170 delegates in 2025 to nearly 300 in 2026, demonstrating ICEF’s growing international reach and recognition within the academic community.

      Empowering Learners Beyond Technological Advancement
      In his opening remarks, Assoc. Prof. Le Hieu Hoc, Dean of the Faculty of Education and Chair of the ICEF 2026 Organising Committee, highlighted the importance of placing learners and their development at the centre of educational transformation.
      “The real question is not simply how we can use new technologies in education, but how we can use them to empower learners — to think critically, learn independently, collaborate across cultures, and make meaningful contributions to society,” he stated.

      He emphasised that technological advancement alone does not guarantee better education. Instead, educational research and innovation must lead to meaningful improvements in learning experiences, educational practices, and outcomes for individuals and communities.
      This perspective set the direction for the conference, encouraging researchers and educators to move beyond technology adoption towards educational innovation grounded in pedagogical principles, research evidence, and real-world impact.
      International Keynotes: Connecting Educational Research with Practice and Impact
      A central highlight of ICEF 2026 was its series of seven keynote presentations by international scholars, offering complementary perspectives on engineering education, teacher professional development, artificial intelligence, learner autonomy, language policy, and educational transformation.
      Developing Engineering Thinking Through Pedagogical Innovation
      Prof. Steffen Kersten from Dresden University of Technology, Germany, delivered a keynote entitled “Typical Engineering Thinking and Consequences for the Methodology of Teaching in Engineering Education.”

      His presentation emphasised that engineering education should develop not only technical knowledge but also the distinctive cognitive processes required in engineering practice, including technical analysis, diagnosis, design, and evaluation. Prof. Kersten highlighted the importance of aligning teaching methods with the ways engineers approach complex technical problems. In particular, he advocated the purposeful use of cognitive learning methods, practical laboratory training, project-based learning, case studies, and technical simulations.
      He also demonstrated how technical diagnostics can be taught through a structured process of system analysis, hypothesis formulation, and verification, allowing students to develop systematic problem-solving and analytical reasoning capabilities. His keynote provided insights into strengthening the connection between academic learning and professional engineering practice.
      Empowering Educators Through Flexible and Collaborative Professional Learning
      Dr. Lizana Oberholzer, Chair of the International Professional Development Association (IPDA), delivered a keynote entitled “Empowering Educators to Enable Learners in the Digital Global Era: What Should Professional Learning Look Like to Enable Others in an Innovative and Fast-Moving Landscape?”
      Her presentation challenged conventional approaches to professional development, particularly training dominated by lengthy presentations and passive knowledge transmission. Drawing on survey findings from educational leaders and teachers, she highlighted a mismatch between how professional development is commonly organised and how educators prefer to learn.

      Dr. Oberholzer advocated flexible, needs-responsive professional learning that gives educators greater autonomy. Through an institutional case study, she illustrated how mentoring, coaching, collaborative research, peer learning, digital resources, podcasts, and personalised learning pathways can support staff motivation, collaboration, and leadership development.
      Her central message was that empowering learners begins with empowering educators: educational leaders should understand teachers’ professional needs, recognise their expertise, and create supportive environments for continuous learning and creativity.
      Combining Artificial Intelligence and Human Guidance in Language Learning
      Assoc. Prof. Ju Seong Lee from The Education University of Hong Kong presented “AI–Human IDLE Coaching: Empowering Learners for Social Impact.”
      His keynote explored how artificial intelligence and human support can enhance Informal Digital Learning of English (IDLE), enabling students to develop language skills through digital activities beyond the classroom. He emphasised the importance of teacher guidance in helping learners plan, undertake, and reflect on self-directed learning, while AI and digital platforms expand opportunities for practice.

      A notable example was an initiative supporting under-resourced schools in Indonesia between January 2024 and May 2025. Through a train-the-trainer approach, the initiative reached 2,820 students across 22 schools. According to the impact figures presented, monthly English-speaking practice time increased by 143%, while reading and listening time increased by 95%. Learners also reported greater enjoyment and improved communication competence.
      These findings illustrated the potential of digitally supported informal learning, combined with teacher development and guidance, to broaden learning opportunities and contribute to educational equity. The Indonesia figures concerned the wider IDLE initiative, rather than the isolated effect of AI.
      Building Sustainable Self-Access Learning Opportunities
      Dr. John R. Baker, affiliated with Ho Chi Minh City University of Industry and Trade, Vietnam, and Shinawatra University, Thailand, delivered a keynote entitled “From Concept to Implementation: Blueprints for Developing Sustainable Self-Access Learning Programs.”

      His presentation focused on how institutions can develop sustainable self-access learning programmes that support learner autonomy and lifelong learning across classroom, institutional, community, and digital environments. Moving beyond concepts, he presented practical considerations for designing, funding, operating, and sustaining initiatives responsive to diverse learners and institutional resources.
      Dr. Baker explored how AI could support the planning of physical and virtual learning spaces, appointment administration, tutor development, educational resources, and reporting. Importantly, he invited educators to consider what AI should augment and what must remain human, highlighting the need for practical, sustainable, learner-centred support systems.
      Rethinking Higher Education Through the Story Model and AI
      Prof. Susan Drake from Brock University, Canada, presented “The Story Model Meets AI in Higher Education,” offering a broader perspective on transforming educational practices and values.
      Using the Story Model framework, she contrasted the “old story” of education, characterised by standardisation, memorisation, passive learning, and teacher-centred instruction, with a “new story” emphasising active inquiry, interdisciplinary learning, authentic assessment, collaboration, and learner agency.

      Rather than treating traditional practice and innovation as opposing alternatives, Prof. Drake proposed a “Both/And” approach that integrates foundational educational values with emerging opportunities. AI can support personalised learning, simulated conversations, and writing development, but learners must critically evaluate generated suggestions, identify biases, verify information, and make informed decisions.
      Her presentation underscored the importance of strengthening human agency, creativity, and critical thinking through responsible human–AI collaboration.
      Making Language Work for Learning: Language Policy and Educational Practice
      Prof. Gina O. Gonong from Philippine Normal University, the Philippines, delivered a keynote entitled “Making Language Work for Learning: Perspectives on Language Policy and Practice in the Philippines.”

      Her topic brought the relationship between language policy, classroom practice, and learning into the conference discussion, with attention to the multilingual context of the Philippines. It invited reflection on how the languages used in education shape learners’ experiences and opportunities to access knowledge.
      The subject is relevant to countries such as Vietnam, where foreign language education, multilingual communication, and international integration remain significant priorities. It broadened ICEF’s discussion beyond digital tools to consider the role of language in connecting learners with knowledge and cultures.
      Architecting Learner Agency in the Global Digital Era
      Prof. Sujatha Mukiri from The English and Foreign Languages University, India, delivered a keynote entitled “Architecting Agency: Empowering Learners for the Global Digital Era.”
      Her topic addressed learner agency, a central theme of ICEF 2026, and invited discussion of how learning environments, teaching practices, and technologies can help students take an active role in shaping their learning. The focus aligns with the transition from teacher-centred knowledge transmission towards learner-centred educational experiences.

      It also provides a lens for considering how learners can develop independence, reflection, responsible decision-making, and meaningful engagement in an increasingly interconnected world.
      Together, the seven keynote presentations offered complementary perspectives on empowering learners in the digital global era. Spanning engineering pedagogy, professional learning, AI, learner autonomy, language policy, and educational transformation, they highlighted the importance of connecting technological innovation with human development, inclusive practices, and sustainable impact.

      A Multidisciplinary Platform for Academic Exchange
      Beyond the keynote presentations, ICEF 2026 featured pre-conference workshops, parallel paper presentations, and poster sessions covering a broad range of contemporary educational research topics.
      The programme included ten thematic parallel sessions addressing AI and digital innovation in education; learner development and agency; teacher professional development; educational leadership and the future workforce; foreign language education; intercultural communication; translation and AI; inclusive education; and innovations in Chinese and Korean language teaching.

      Pre-conference activities also offered professional development opportunities, including workshops on AI-mediated academic writing and publishing in leading academic journals in the AI era. These activities enabled participants to share research, examine emerging practices, and explore interdisciplinary partnerships.
      Strengthening HUST’s Internationalisation Through Academic Partnerships
      ICEF 2026 represents an important contribution to HUST’s internationalisation strategy, emphasising academic partnerships, collaborative research, and engagement with the global academic community.
      The conference was co-organised with the International Professional Development Association (IPDA), The Education University of Hong Kong, the University of Glasgow, the Academy of Policy and Development, the Diplomatic Academy of Vietnam, Nguyen Tat Thanh University, Phenikaa University, and Bac Nha Institute for Applied Linguistics Research.

      In his opening address, Assoc. Prof. Le Hieu Hoc emphasised that internationalisation involves more than expanding institutional networks. It creates opportunities for students, academics, and partners to exchange knowledge, develop new perspectives, and jointly address challenges extending beyond national borders. “ICEF is a concrete example of this commitment in action,” he noted.
      The close collaboration between FED and FFL has been central to the establishment and continued development of ICEF since its first edition in 2024. Bringing together expertise in educational sciences, educational technology, engineering education, teacher development, linguistics, and foreign language education, the two faculties have developed an interdisciplinary platform linking researchers, educators, and institutions across countries.
      Parallel and Poster Sessions: Diverse Perspectives and Emerging Research
      The afternoon of 23 October featured two rounds of parallel presentations alongside four poster sessions, providing opportunities for researchers to present their work, engage in scholarly discussions, and receive feedback from colleagues across disciplines and institutions.
      The first round covered five areas: AI and digital innovation in education; learner development, agency, and educational experience; teacher development, professional identity, and reflective practice; digital innovation in foreign language education; and Chinese language education and teaching innovation in the AI era.

      The second round explored educational leadership, higher education, and the future workforce; language, culture, and intercultural communication; translation, AI, and language professional practices; inclusive education and learner experience; and technological innovations and learner empowerment in Korean language teaching.
      Complementing the oral presentations, four poster sessions offered a setting for researchers to share emerging findings, discuss methodological approaches, and exchange ideas with participants. Together, the afternoon sessions highlighted the breadth of research represented at ICEF 2026 and encouraged connections between educational sciences, language teaching, and digital innovation.

      From Insights and Innovations to Lasting Educational Impact
      ICEF’s expanding participation and partnerships demonstrate its potential as a sustainable academic network for educational research and innovation. For FED, it offers opportunities to strengthen joint research and publications, academic exchanges, and collaboration in educational sciences, educational technology, and teacher development.

      The conference reinforced a central message: the future of education is not simply about adopting new technologies, but about empowering educators and learners to use knowledge, innovation, and technology to create positive and sustainable change.
      Building on three consecutive editions, ICEF will continue to serve as a bridge connecting scholars, educators, and institutions worldwide, advancing educational sciences and foreign language teaching in the digital global era.
