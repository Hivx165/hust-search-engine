A note of thanks

With gratitude from the Organizing Committee

On 2 October 2026, at Hotel du Parc Hanoi, the 8th International Conference on Quality Management and Food Safety (QMFS 2026) came to a successful close. Organized by the School of Chemistry and Life Sciences, Hanoi University of Science and Technology, together with VinaCert, the University of Greenwich, the University of Kent and LASE University Group, the conference was one of the flagship events celebrating the 70th anniversary of HUST (1956 – 2026).

We sincerely thank every delegate, guest, speaker and scientist, from Vietnam and abroad, who gave their time, knowledge and perspective. Your presence turned QMFS 2026 into a truly interdisciplinary forum where regulators, researchers and industry sat at the same table for safer, more sustainable food.

Our special thanks go to the Vietnam Food Administration (Ministry of Health), the British Embassy Hanoi, The Academy of Medical Sciences (UK), our sponsors and industry partners, the Scientific Committee, the session chairs, and the staff, lecturers and student volunteers who looked after every detail of the day.

With warm regards,

QMFS 2026 Organizing Committee

School of Chemistry and Life Sciences – Hanoi University of Science and Technology

The day in review

One day, many milestones

From the opening ceremony to the gala dinner, here is how 2 October 2026 unfolded.

01 08:30

Opening & policy outlook

Prof. Le Anh Tuan, President of HUST, opened the conference, stressing that food quality and safety are increasingly tied to public health and to the competitiveness of Vietnamese agriculture. Welcome remarks came from Mr. Matthew Albon-Crouch (British Embassy Hanoi) and Dr. Chu Quoc Thinh, Director of the Vietnam Food Administration, who then presented new food safety policies in Vietnam.

02 09:10

Plenary: six keynote lectures

Experts from Thailand, the United Kingdom, Japan and Vietnam spoke on circular food systems, food safety risk management in the AI era, sustainable protein from rice side-streams, building human capability, and metabolic engineering outside living cells.

Our keynote speakers
03 13:30

Six parallel sessions

More than 40 talks ran across six tracks, linking research, regulation and industry practice.

  • 1Quality Management & Digital Transformation
  • 2Biotechnology: Research to Practice
  • 3Plant-Based Protein Food & Beverages
  • 4Quality of Natural Products
  • 5Education & Human Resources
  • 6Probiotics & Food System Transformation
04 17:00

Student posters & awards

The poster area was full of work by young researchers, and the best student teams were recognized at the award ceremony that closed the scientific programme.

05 18:00

Gala dinner & new partnerships

The evening saw the launch of the Plant Protein Plus Network UK–SE Asia (3PUKSEA) and the Food Technology Universities Network, the signing of strategic cooperation agreements, and the AI-crafted beer that celebrates HUST's 70th anniversary.

Key takeaways

Messages that stay with us

01

Risk-based, data-driven management

New legislation puts risk-based control across the whole supply chain, from production to distribution, supported by digitalization and a unified national food safety database.

Dr. Chu Quoc Thinh – Vietnam Food Administration
02

AI and “digital chemical fingerprints”

Artificial intelligence can support risk management, while spectral data combined with IoT and blockchain can build digital chemical fingerprints to detect food fraud.

Prof. Pham Quoc Long – VANPS
03

Circular economy & agricultural by-products

Climate change, population growth and antimicrobial resistance call for better use of agricultural by-products, such as extracting protein from rice side-streams through UK–Southeast Asia partnerships.

Prof. Anil Kumar Anal (AIT) & Dr. Parag Acharya (NRI)
